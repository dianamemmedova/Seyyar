Local food guide

Azerbaijani cuisine: what to try

From saffron-infused Shah Plov to crispy herb qutabs and unhurried tea rituals—here is how to order with confidence in Baku.

Browse Baku restaurants

Six classic dishes to order first

Shah Plov

Saffron rice layered with chestnuts, dried fruits, and lamb baked inside a golden crisp bread crust.

18–26 ₼ (≈ $11–15) Where to try

Yarpaq Dolması

Bite-sized grape leaves stuffed with seasoned lamb and rice, served with cool yogurt.

12–18 ₼ (≈ $7–11) Where to try

Qutab

Thin crescent flatbreads filled with fresh greens, pumpkin, or minced meat, dusted with sumac.

2–4 ₼ each (≈ $1.50) Where to try

Sheki Piti

Slow-cooked clay-pot stew of lamb, chickpeas, and chestnuts eaten in two steps: broth first, then mashed.

14–18 ₼ (≈ $8–11) Where to try

Lülə & Tikə Kebab

Charcoal-grilled minced lamb and tender cuts served wrapped in warm lavash with sumac onions.

14–22 ₼ (≈ $8–13) Where to try

Baku Pakhlava

Diamond-cut pastry layered with walnuts, cardamom, and saffron honey—always paired with black tea.

6–10 ₼ (≈ $4–6) Where to try

Tea culture (Çayxana)

Black tea from Lankaran is served in pear-shaped armudu glasses that keep the tea hot at the bottom and comfortable to sip at the rim. Locals place a cube of sugar or a spoonful of white cherry jam (mürəbbə) alongside rather than stirring it in.

Food etiquette tips

  • Meals are meant to be shared—order 2–3 starters for the table before mains.
  • Fresh bread (təndir çörəyi) and herbs are treated with care; never place bread upside down.
  • Service charge (5–10%) is sometimes included on the bill; if not, 10% in cash is appreciated.
